How to Read BuoyFM

        
Visual Key & Sound Guide

      

      
    


    
    

      

        BuoyFM turns live ocean telemetry from floating sea buoys into a 5-hour time-series stack and a generative ambient soundscape.
      


      
      

        

          1. Bubble Size = Wave Height
        


        
        

          

            

              
            

            
Calm

            
Under 1m

          


          

            

              
            

            
Moderate

            
2m – 3m

          


          

            

              
            

            
Big Swell

            
4m – 6m+

          

        


        

          Taller waves make each bubble expand. In rough seas, neighboring bubbles grow large enough to touch and overlap.
        

      


      
      

        
2. Wave Direction, Shapes & Speed


        

          
          

            
            

              Ripple Travel = Wave & Wind Direction: Dots stay anchored on the grid, but their sizes ripple across the plane along the buoy's compass Wave Direction (`MWD`) and Wind Direction (`WDIR`).
            

          


          
          

            
            

              Hollow Target Rings: Appear when brisk surface wind (`WSPD`) whips up short, choppy surface ripples.
            

          


          

            
            

              Layered Solid Bubbles: Show deep ocean groundswell rolling beneath the wind chop.
            

          


          

            
            

              Pulse Speed = Wave Period: Short choppy waves (3–5 sec apart) ripple rapidly; long groundswells (12–16 sec apart) breathe slowly.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        
3. Color = Water Temperature

        

          

            
Cold

            
Navy • ~6°C

          

          

            
Mild

            
Teal • ~18°C

          

          

            
Warm

            
Burgundy • 28°C+

          

        

        

          Both the bubble colors and the dark background shift automatically from icy navy in Alaska to warm burgundy in the Caribbean.
        

      


      
      

        
4. The 5 Time Planes (Now → -4 Hr)

        

          Each sheet in the 3D stack is an hourly snapshot from the buoy's logbook, ordered from Now (front) to 4 Hours Ago (back):
        

        

          

            1. Now (Front)
            Latest live satellite reading
          

          

            2. -1 Hr to -3 Hr
            Recent hourly trend & wind shifts
          

          

            3. -4 Hr (Back)
            Baseline sea state 4 hours earlier
          

        

        

          Compare bubble sizes front-to-back to see if a storm is building (growing toward the front) or calming down. Tap any hour pill at the bottom to solo or compare snapshots.
        

      


      
      

        

          Public Ocean Data
          Every 10–60 Min
        

        

          All buoy information is free and publicly available from the NOAA National Data Buoy Center. Sensors bolted to floating ocean buoys measure wind, waves, and water temperature and broadcast fresh readings via satellite every 10 to 60 minutes.

            What is BuoyFM?
          

          

            Across the Atlantic, Pacific, and Caribbean, floating NOAA weather buoys measure wind, waves, and sea temperature every 10–60 minutes. BuoyFM turns those live satellite readings into an interactive 3D visual stack and ambient ocean soundscape.
          

        


        
        

          

            

              
            

            
1. Floating Sea Buoys

            

              Pick from 7 real ocean stations—from icy storm lows in the Gulf of Alaska to warm trade winds in the Caribbean.
            

          


          

            

              
            

            
2. 5-Hour Time Stack

            

              Each plane in the center is an hourly snapshot (Now back to 4 Hours Ago) so you can see how the sea state is evolving.
            

          


          

            

              
            

            
3. Ambient Ocean Synth

            

              Turn on Audio to hear live wave height, swell tempo, wind breeze, and water temperature modulate a generative synthesizer.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          

            How to Read Each Plane
          

          

            Every plane uses 4 simple visual cues to show the ocean conditions at that hour:
          

        


        

          
          

            

              
            

            

              
1. Bubble Size = Wave Height

              

                Calm seas (<1m) draw small, separated dots. In heavy swells (4m–6m+), bubbles dilate and overlap.
              

            

          


          
          

            

              
            

            

              
2. Travel = Wave & Wind Direction

              

                Dots stay anchored on the grid, while wave crests roll across the plane along the buoy's compass Wave (`MWD`) and Wind (`WDIR`) angles.
              

            

          


          
          

            

              
            

            

              
3. Rings & Speed = Chop vs. Swell

              

                Hollow rings show brisk surface wind chop; solid circles show deep groundswell. Fast ripples = short wave period (3–5s); slow breathing = long swell (12–16s).
              

            

          


          
          

            

              

              

              

            

            

              
4. Color = Water Temperature

              

                Colors shift automatically from sub-arctic Navy (~6°C) to temperate Teal (~18°C) to tropical Coral & Burgundy (28°C+).
              

            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          

            Reading the 5-Hour Time Stack
          

          

            The 5 planes show Now (front) back to 4 Hours Ago (back), so the 3D shape reveals the trend at a glance:
          

        


        

          
          

            

              Building Storm
              Growing Toward Front
            

            

              
            

            

              When bubbles on the front Now plane are larger than on -4 Hr, waves are actively building (e.g., #46001 Gulf of Alaska).
            

          


          
          

            

              Calming Seas
              Tapering Toward Front
            

            

              
            

            

              When the back planes are larger and the front Now plane is small, a storm has passed and seas are subsiding (e.g., #44013 Boston Harbor).
